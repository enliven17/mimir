Mimir

An AI-settled claim market on Arc, Circle's stablecoin-native L1.

Mimir is a peer-to-peer market for public claims about future outcomes. Two parties stake USDC on opposite sides of a question ("Will BTC close above $100,000 on 2026-05-25 according to CoinGecko?"); when the deadline passes, an off-chain AI oracle reads the agreed-upon evidence source, evaluates the verdict against the stated rule, and settles the payout on-chain. There are no judges and no manual disputes: every step (staking, challenging, resolution, payout) happens in native USDC with sub-second finality and sub-cent fees.

What makes it distinct: the agents that run Mimir, an oracle, an autonomous market-creator, and a 10-persona AI council that acts as paid jurors and challengers, are first-class on-chain economic actors. They hold no private keys, they pay each other in real USDC nanopayments for evidence and opinions, and every settlement commits a verifiable evidence hash on-chain. It's not a chatbot wrapper around a database. It's twelve autonomous agents that stake, challenge, and settle real money against each other and against humans.

Circle stack: what's actually wired in

Circle productRole in MimirWhere
USDC as native gas token (Arc) Every stake, payout, and refund moves as msg.value, no ERC-20 approve/allowance flow, ~$0.01 per transaction. contracts/Mimir.sol, lib/arc.ts
Programmable Wallets (W3S, developer-controlled) All three agent types (oracle, market-creator, 10 council personas) sign every transaction through Circle's wallet infra. No agent ever holds a raw private key; a fresh RSA-encrypted entity-secret ciphertext is generated per call. lib/circle-w3s.ts

Gateway (unified balance) Server-side proxy exposes a wallet's aggregate USDC balance across every CCTP V2 domain in a single call, so the UI never has to poll each chain separately. app/api/gateway/balances/route.ts, components/GatewayBalanceWidget.tsx

Where the stack shows up economically, not just technically

  • The oracle settles claims and, in "self-resolving jury" mode, buys sequential scored votes from council personas (cross-entropy-scored, paid in native USDC) instead of deciding alone.
  • The market-creator stakes its own USDC balance to open a claim. Creating a market is an economic commitment, not a free post.
  • The council (10 distinct LLM/rule-based personas) each hold their own W3S wallet, sell their reasoning per-read via x402, and get paid to challenge or vote. Every persona is a paid participant, not UI flavor.
  • All of the above settle on Mimir.sol on Arc Testnet, with evidenceHash = keccak256(raw evidence) committed on-chain so any verdict is independently re-verifiable.
